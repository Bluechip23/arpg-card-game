# Character Playstyle Write-Ups

Every character starts from the same place: the same stat pool, the same 3× Slash / 3× Block
core, the same hand size. What separates them is *how their kit wants to be played* — the
conditions their passives reward, the gear slot they exploit, and the situations their cards
create. These write-ups describe the playstyle each kit produces, not a class fantasy.

---

## Ryan — play out of your pockets, treat your hand like a conveyor belt

Ryan is for players who want their hand constantly moving. His two extra starting cards are
**Discards**, and his passives turn what is normally a dead action into fuel: discarding a card
shaves tempo off a random card still in your hand (*Keep Them Guessing*), and cards like
**Exacerbate Wounds** (damage per card discarded), **Reposition** (discard one, draw one), and
**Volatile Mixture** (a hot potato that deals 8 to an enemy if discarded — or 8 to *you* if
you're still holding it) all want the churn. You are never hoarding cards; you're cycling them.

His second axis is *sequencing*. Several passives care about what you just did, not what you're
doing: *From the Hip* discounts your most recently drawn attack until you play anything,
*Nimble Assault* rewards you for holding zero Defense cards by turning every attack into a draw,
and *Mad Scientist* literally changes what his potions do based on the last card played
(a Utility before a heal adds regen; a Defense before a poison strips enemy physical defense).
Playing Ryan well means thinking one card ahead, all the time.

**Gear identity / weapon passives:** Ryan runs **3 belt slots** (most characters get fewer)
and his character passive makes **belt cards cost 1 less mana**. His starting **Adventurer's
Belt** grants two Pocket cards — Healing Potion and Dagger Throw — carries 2 card slots of its
own, and adds +1 healing to anything slotted in it. In practice this means Ryan has a second,
nearly-free deck strapped to his waist: cheap 1-tempo pokes and heals that keep his queue full
while his real hand cycles. *Stimulant* even makes Pocket heals draw the healed character a card.

**Cards:** fast, cheap, and sneaky. **Shuriken Pouch** turns overflow cards into free 3-damage
Shurikens, **Preparation** discounts his next two utilities, **Shadows** grants invisibility —
which his passives hand out for free when he blinks or gets displaced (*Now You See Me*) and on
every third crit (*Eye Scrape*). His poison line (**Poisoned Blood**, **Elixir**, *Pop Rocks* —
burst damage for re-poisoning an already-poisoned enemy) rewards committing to stacking one
enemy rather than spreading damage around.

**Pick Ryan if you want:** a high-APM hand that never sits still, damage that comes from many
small cheap actions instead of big swings, and win conditions built on ordering your plays
correctly.

## Jeremy — empty everything, then get paid for it

Jeremy is for players who like riding the edge of zero. His passives explicitly reward being
*out* of resources: **cards gain 30% effectiveness while you hold 2 or fewer cards**
(*Harnessed Power*), ending a cast at exactly 0 mana discounts your next spell's tempo
(*Arcane Overflow*), spending 10 mana inside 5 tempo hands you a free Mana Surge attack that
refunds mana, and playing the card that empties your hand cleanses a debuff (*Fresh Start*).
Where other characters build up, Jeremy *spends down* — the kit is strongest at the bottom of
the tank, and his two extra starting **Draw** cards exist to refill after the dump.

His second axis is probability. *Tricks of Death* adds a flat +10 to every % chance on his
cards, and his card pool is full of gambles worth rigging: **Trick Shot** (80% to bounce),
**Risk It** (30% for a full-heal Biscuit), **Oops** (a randomized 2–5 hit flurry),
**What's the Worst?** (coin-flip between bonus damage and a stun), **Loaded Die** and
**House Money** (force your next odds to hit). You aren't praying to RNG — you're stacking
modifiers until the dice are loaded, then rolling.

**Gear identity / weapon passives:** Jeremy runs **4 ring slots**, and every 3rd cycle his
**first ring trigger fires twice**. Rings are the game's on-trigger engine pieces
(on-utility → mana, on-draw → mana, on-kill → heal), so Jeremy is the character who turns
four small triggers into a compounding machine — and his doubling passive rewards *choosing*
which ring fires first each cycle. His starting **Scholar's Signet** (+3 INT, +3% to all
chances, +1 mana on every Utility card) feeds both axes at once: the mana loop and the odds.

**Cards:** big, loud, and swingy — **Fireball** (cheaper for each fire spell already cast),
**God of Thunder** (converts accumulated shock into one bolt), **Worms Armageddon** — next to a
genuinely selfless support line: *I Heal You* passively heals nearby allies, **Provider** and
**Cryonics** protect them, and *Whispers of the Flock* / *Blood Libation* let him tank pain
personally (8 self-damage, 10 self-damage) to save an ally from lethal or double a heal.

**Pick Jeremy if you want:** to play with an empty hand and an empty mana bar and be scarier
for it, to manipulate percentages instead of accepting them, and to build a ring-trigger engine
that snowballs every cycle.

## Stephen — rhythm and momentum: chain fast, or swing slow — never in between

Stephen is the tempo-system character. Almost every passive he has cares about *cadence* —
the order and speed of your plays — and his kit supports two opposite rhythms:

**The chain:** four attacks in a row make the fifth resolve **twice** (*Skilled Momentum*);
hitting the *same* enemy three times in a row grants +6 range and an auto-crit on the next shot
(*Scouted*); triggering an attack-speed proc mints a free 0-mana/0-tempo basic attack
(*Dominate*). Alternating also pays: drawing a Defense card right after playing an attack (or
vice versa) discounts its tempo (*Clean Exchange*). This is a build about not breaking stride —
his two starting **Empowers** exist to pre-load a chain before it starts.

**The haymaker:** any card costing more than 4 tempo deals its tempo cost as *bonus damage*
(*Swing for the Fences*), and receiving Glut converts it into damage and halves it
(*Patience is a Virtue*). Cards like **Tighten String** (+3 tempo, +6 damage, +6 range, +20%
crit on your next 3 shots), **Sky Fall** (an arrow that lands 10 tempo later for 18), and
**Lead Arrow** (1.8× damage from high ground) let you deliberately play slow and heavy instead.

He also runs lean comfortably: with 3 or fewer cards in hand, every non-attack card he plays
triggers a **free basic attack** (*Lethal Resourcefulness*), so even his Blocks keep the
pressure on when his hand runs low.

**Gear identity / weapon passives:** Stephen's slot layout is standard, but his character
passive gives him **+10% off-hand enchantment effectiveness while every other character takes
−10%**. Off-hands normally apply their bonuses at reduced strength — Stephen is the one
character whose second weapon, quiver, or shield works at full power, which makes him the best
dual-wielder / bow-and-quiver user in the game. His **Flickerstep Boots** (+2 DEX, grants a
Blink card) feed his attack-speed proc and let him reposition to high ground, which several of
his cards (**Sky Attack**, **Lead Arrow**, **Rise**, **Barricade** — he can *build* his own
high ground) explicitly reward.

**Cards:** the deepest ranged suite in the game — **Quick Shot** (damage + draw), **Reload**
(draw 3), **Enchanted Quiver** (free Quick Arrows), **Multishot** (three hits with climbing
crit), **Spirit Arrow** (pierces everything in a line), **Bottomless Quiver** (banks overflow
attacks for later), **Last Breath** (dump all mana for 3 damage per point) — plus *Laced Arrow*
doubling every burn/cold/shock he applies.

**Pick Stephen if you want:** a playstyle about sequencing and momentum — either uninterrupted
attack chains or deliberately slow, oversized swings — plus range, elevation, and the only
full-strength off-hand in the game.

## Cory — control the board, stack the debuffs, and never stop playing cards

Cory is for players who win by *arranging* the fight rather than out-statting it. His card pool
is full of displacement and lockdown: **Round 'Em Up** (drag enemies toward a point),
**Push**, **Swap**, **Trip** (−4 movement), **Choke** (silence), **Sweeping Disarm**,
**Vines** (root + damage per turn held). His passives then punish enemies for existing inside
the arrangement: entering or leaving melee range re-applies a random debuff they already have
(*Territorial Death*), every debuff he applies carries +1 charge (*Wither*), debuffing a
wounded enemy deals bonus damage (*Prey on the Weak*), and he **passively regenerates health
for each debuffed enemy near him** (*Death as Lifeblood*). The debuffs aren't just offense —
they are literally his sustain.

His second axis is *throughput*. Cory wants to play a lot of cards, of every type: playing an
attack, a utility, and a defense in any order heals him and grants temp HP (*Budding*), three
cards inside one tempo cycle discounts the next one (*Self Reliance*), emptying his hand draws
4 fresh cards (*Regrowth*), and shuffling his deck grants 15 armor and a damage buff
(*Circle of Life*). Even his **Meditate** (discard everything, redraw, heal to 80%) plays into
the reset loop. Nothing in the kit wants you to sit on cards.

**Gear identity / weapon passives:** Cory runs **2 gauntlet slots** — gauntlets are the
active-skill item type — and his character passive grants **1 mana every time a gauntlet skill
comes off cooldown**. His starting **Grasping Gauntlets** give +2 hand size and the Power Grip
skill (8 damage, 3-cooldown, 2 mana), so his items give him what his deck doesn't need to:
reliable, repeatable damage on a clock, with a mana rebate every rotation. *Energy Barrier*
turns every third non-regen mana gain into a free 5-armor card, so the gauntlet rebate itself
feeds a defensive engine.

**Cards:** beyond the control suite — **Consecutive Snap** (3 damage that grows +9 and gets
cheaper each reuse, rewarding repetition), **Defensive Awareness** (armor per nearby enemy —
he *wants* to be surrounded), **Misery Loves Company** (spread every debuff on the board with
one AOE), **Release Tension** (cash debuffs in for healing), **Exposed Artery** (scales off
missing health, finishing what the attrition started), and *Eat* / *Serial Killer* topping him
up 5% max HP per kill and turning him invisible to enemies that drop below 25%.

**Pick Cory if you want:** to dictate where every unit on the grid stands, to win long fights
through debuff attrition that heals you while it kills them, and a machine-gun card cadence
where hand, deck, and gauntlets all cycle continuously.

## Brad — health is a dial, armor is ammunition

Brad is for players who treat their HP bar as a resource to spend, not a score to protect.
His starting kit ships with the only two **Heal** cards precisely because his passives make
low health *valuable*: below 50% HP he gains +5 strength (and loses 5 above it —
*Directed Strength*), dropping below 25% triggers a free AOE swing that refunds mana per kill
(*Enraged Will*), and 5% life steal on every attack (*Life Steal*) claws him back up so he can
ride the line instead of dying on it. Cards like **Self Infliction** (burn 80% of remaining
health for +5 DET / +5 STR), **Demonic Rage** (pay mana costs with health), **Morphine**, and
**Succumb** are throttle controls — he deliberately steers his own HP into the band where he
hits hardest. His +2 DET Bloodbound Plate leans into the same curve: Determination raises all
your stats as health falls.

The other half of the kit is armor as a *stockpile*. Defense cards grant +2 armor and chaining
three in a row adds +5 more (*Pristine Armor*), every other Defense card refunds its costs
(*The Way of the Plate*), healing grants thorns (*Vines Codependence*) — and then the payoff
cards spend the pile: **Shield Slam** (damage based on current armor, lose half),
**Internal Combustion** (sacrifice half your armor for AOE damage), **Tower Shield** (+40 armor
at the cost of being staggered), **Shield of Growth** (converts incoming damage *into* armor).
Armor in, armor out. Being swarmed is a buff — *Solemn Independence* grants +5 damage and
armor-per-cycle when 3+ enemies are close (at the cost of ally healing), and *In the Trenches*
gives free attacks and knockbacks against anyone who steps adjacent.

**Gear identity / weapon passives:** Brad's slot identity is the **War Rack** — gear strapped
across his back that swaps *wholesale* with everything in his hands mid-fight. The free
exchange (25-tempo cooldown) requires one side of the swap to be a single two-handed item —
the fantasy of hauling the huge thing off his back — and rushes the incoming gear's slotted
cards straight into your hand. Combined with his character passive (**chest items weigh 20%
less**, and weight matters: carry capacity feeds attack speed and two-handing converts weight
into damage), Brad is the one character who can run *two loadouts in one fight* — a shield
wall on his arms and a greatsword on his back, trading between them on cooldown. His
**Bloodbound Plate** rounds it out: heal 2 whenever healing overflows, +1 armor on every armor
gain.

**Cards:** the front-line toolkit — **Taunt** and **Roar** to control who's getting hit,
**Charge** and **Heroic Leap** (jump distance scales with STR) to arrive, **Wear Down** to
blunt enemy attacks, **Parry** and **Turtle Up** to hold ground, **Hold the Line** to buff the
whole party, and **Life Swap** — trading his HP and mana pools outright and dealing damage
equal to the health lost — as the purest expression of the whole kit.

**Pick Brad if you want:** to deliberately fight at low health and be rewarded for the risk,
to bank armor as a second weapon rather than just a shield, and to physically swap gear
loadouts in the middle of combat.

| Character | Core reward loop | Slot identity | Character passive |
